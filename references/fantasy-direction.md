# Secondary-world fantasy direction

## Definition

An original world has its own stable laws. Things impossible in reality exist here natively, consistently, and as ordinary life. They are not exceptions to real-world rules or temporary special events. There is no need to explain their origin, mechanism, or the world's history.

Ground the impossible phenomenon in concrete things or experiences the listener can easily understand. Its motion, changes, and relationship with its surroundings reveal consistent rules. Do not grant it unexpected new powers or alter its limits midway.

## A single-episode outline

Each theme defines one world and one episode through six fields:

1. Title: an English working title and Chinese equivalent with a sense of atmosphere, avoiding a stiff explanatory label.
2. Core fantasy: the episode's indispensable, distinctive feature. It has character or vitality and supports substantial interaction or natural change. Removing it would make the main experience cease to work.
3. Core experience: summarize what the listener experiences, then describe its continuous chronological development. Changes in location, scenery, and sensation revolve around the core fantasy and gradually become quieter.
4. Time and environment: specify the time, starting point, and principal spaces. These must support both the fantasy's perceptibility and a comfortable experience. Do not mechanically require nighttime.
5. Key rules: include only the episode's relevant motion, changes, scope, and limits. Do not explain mechanisms.
6. Final resting state: specify the listener's final location and surroundings. Add no new events that renew attention.

The theme defines what to write. Script Parts, exercises, pauses, and sound markup belong to the writing stage. Execute `fantasy-topic-prompt.md` when generating themes.

## Check and confirm

Check that the core fantasy is ordinary within its world and readily understandable. Its experience, environment, rules, and ending must agree and sustain a complete, quiet episode. Candidates should differ visibly from existing works; do not adapt an example's core fantasy into a supposedly new theme.

The user selects and confirms the theme. Apply their must-keep details, identified problems, and requested direction. If the core fantasy changes, revise all affected fields together. Do not begin full writing without confirmation. Use the same confirmed outline directly, without stripping out or reorganizing away necessary information.

## Example: The Ship at Twilight

Core fantasy: Vespera, a wooden ferry serving mountaintop harbors, is the residents' ordinary way to travel through the sky.

Core experience: arrive at the harbor at dusk, enter a warm, spacious cabin, and settle. As the ship slowly departs and rises, hills, villages, and lights recede. Clouds soften the view; above them, evening turns to night. Scenery and activity diminish while the listener stays seated during steady travel.

Time and environment: a mountaintop harbor at dusk, followed by the cabin, the air above the valley, the cloud layer, and the sky above it.

Key rules: the ferry operates routinely, without visible wings, and rises and travels smoothly. Passengers perceive changing surroundings from the cabin. They need not reach a destination or help the crew accomplish anything.

Final resting state: the listener remains seated in the warm cabin as the ship travels steadily above the clouds, with less light and fewer visible details.

This illustrates a complete experience, not a template for new themes. The full script is in `../examples/the-ship-at-twilight.en.md`; individual details do not override current writing rules.
